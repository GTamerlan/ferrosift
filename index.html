Methods, assumptions & design status

Historical conveyor comparison. The selected Ferrosift YOLO26s configuration detected 7,081 of 7,535 visible-copper annotations (93.9748%); the YOLO11s reference detected 6,448 (85.5740%). The difference is 633 annotation detections, or 8.4008 percentage points, across 1,522 development frames. Model configuration and thresholds were selected using this cohort. Repeated appearances of the same piece count as repeated annotations; this is development evidence, not a fresh independent holdout or a measure of unique recovered objects.

Operating point and comparison scope. Candidate and reference use frozen thresholds of 0.8073331713676453 and 0.9101877808570862. Candidate copper alarms matched 361 steel annotations and left 3 unmatched alarms; the reference matched 364 steel annotations and left 1 unmatched alarm. Their respective totals of 364 and 365 non-copper alarms are each approximately 1% of 36,563 steel annotations. This conservative development alarm-burden cap is distinct from conventional false-positive rate and physical steel loss. The comparison reflects complete selected configurations: architecture, training and selection effects are not isolated. The reference is an internal model, not a commercial sorter. Recorded counts and provenance · Evaluation methods.

Video interpretation. Source footage, author overlays and Ferrosift predictions are identified separately in the video comparison. Historical SteelDS source exposure and repeated object pools remain relevant. Playback speed, sampled prediction cadence and measured inference speed are separate quantities. New material types, camera views, lighting, occlusion and belt loads require their own evaluation; detection results do not measure recovered mass, purity or ejection success.

Engineering design and capture hold. The CAD is preliminary and not for fabrication. The current receiving geometry has an unresolved capture hold: a gravity-only 1.5 m/s outer-lane point path clears a converging wall by about 70 mm. Receiving containment must be revised and physically tested before an operating speed or full-width capture route can be approved. Actuator selection, sensing coverage, control timing, guarding and recovery trials are the next engineering steps. Viewer motion is a design illustration. Capture analysis · Design holds.

Planning scenario. The operator calculator uses the assumptions entered by the reader. It explores feed, recovery, price and operating-cost scenarios; its outputs are estimates rather than measured savings. The engineering program describes teams and work we plan to build.
