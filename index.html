RESEARCH & DEVELOPMENTFUND THE EVIDENCE

TURN A HYPOTHESIS
INTO A USEFUL TOOL.

Research funding will support the instruments, engineering and independent trials needed to decide whether this module earns a place on a recycling line.

01

ESTABLISH THE MEASUREMENT

Build and characterize the paired sensing heads, synchronized acquisition and camera calibration. Measure how geometry, belt background, nearby metal and temperature affect the signals.

Evidence to produce: repeatable measurements and a mapped useful aperture.

02

TEST THE ADDED INFORMATION

Compare directions, frequencies and full passage sequences against strong simpler baselines on independent fragments and source lots, with material analysis and buyer-defined classes.

Evidence to produce: a reproducible improvement on a valuable sorting decision.

03

PROVE THE INTEGRATION

Engineer maintainable mounts, calibration and host interfaces. Then test the collected output, operating burden and net value with line owners and integrators.

Evidence to produce: practical deployment conditions and operator economics.

Each stage will earn the next. A stronger induction response alone will not establish copper purity or total value; buyer requirements and the composition of the complete fragment will determine the useful route.

Methods, assumptions & design status

Historical conveyor comparison. The selected Ferrosift YOLO26s configuration detected 7,081 of 7,535 visible-copper annotations (93.9748%); the YOLO11s reference detected 6,448 (85.5740%). The difference is 633 annotation detections, or 8.4008 percentage points, across 1,522 development frames. Model configuration and thresholds were selected using this cohort. Repeated appearances of the same piece count as repeated annotations; this is development evidence, not a fresh independent holdout or a measure of unique recovered objects.

Operating point and comparison scope. Candidate and reference use frozen thresholds of 0.8073331713676453 and 0.9101877808570862. Candidate copper alarms matched 361 steel annotations and left 3 unmatched alarms; the reference matched 364 steel annotations and left 1 unmatched alarm. Their respective totals of 364 and 365 non-copper alarms are each approximately 1% of 36,563 steel annotations. This conservative development alarm-burden cap is distinct from conventional false-positive rate and physical steel loss. The comparison reflects complete selected configurations: architecture, training and selection effects are not isolated. The reference is an internal model, not a commercial sorter. Recorded counts and provenance · Evaluation methods.

Video interpretation. Source footage, author overlays and Ferrosift predictions are identified separately in the video comparison. Historical SteelDS source exposure and repeated object pools remain relevant. Playback speed, sampled prediction cadence and measured inference speed are separate quantities. New material types, camera views, lighting, occlusion and belt loads require their own evaluation; detection results do not measure recovered mass, purity or ejection success.

Module research and design status. The combined module is a development hypothesis. Testing will compare simpler configurations on independent pieces and source lots, then assess material analysis, collected purity, recovery and net value. Induction will support routing rather than direct elemental assay. CAD dimensions, coil layouts and installation envelopes are proposed; the model is preliminary and not fabrication-certified. The 180 mm lane is a mechanical test passage, not measured sensor coverage. The initial 12.5–50 mm feed target requires a separate height, mass and preparation specification.

Installation and timing. Two separated induction stations observe a fragment sequentially. Belt encoder counts do not measure fragment slip, roll or bounce; object association, calibration, useful field coverage, cross-talk, actual belt and host-metal effects, loaded clearances and downstream timing require tests. The module excludes host feeding, ejection and collection equipment. Engineering holds · Host interfaces.

Planning scenario. The walkthrough’s fixed example uses hypothetical feed, copper content, physical recovery, payable value and operating cost. It is not measured savings, and no CV score contributes dollars. The original reader-assumption calculator remains available separately. The engineering program describes teams and work we plan to build.

1. Global e-waste. UNITAR / ITU, Global E-waste Monitor 2024, reporting 2022 estimates: 62 million tonnes generated, 22.3% formally collected and recycled, and US$62 billion in recoverable natural resources left unaccounted for. We calculate 77.7% as 100% minus 22.3%; tonnage is rounded to one decimal. The dollar figure is resource value, not audited company losses.

2. Company examples. TOMRA’s PNW Metal case study (May 2021) and Premier Surplus case study (March 2021). The fourfold figure refers to Zorba product offerings, not total recovery or throughput. The article’s 75–90% figure is a consultant expectation; about 95% is the operator’s report. Neither is a universal baseline or multilayer-PCB copper-recovery rate. The hypothetical operator example uses independent assumptions. First-pass recovery does not establish final disposal rates.

S1–S4. Sensing precedents. [S1] O’Toole et al., magnetic induction spectroscopy (2018) studied selected homogeneous metals on a moving belt with geometry features. [S2] Williams et al., induction and machine vision (2024) studied aluminium with offline classification and corrected image matching. [S3] Li et al., PCB eddy-current separation (2017) concerns force-based separation, not spectroscopy classification. [S4] Chomsuwan et al., double-layer PCB inspection (2005) used close-probe, slow scans of engineered boards. These are relevant ingredients, not validation of Ferrosift’s module on irregular PCB fragments.

S5–S6. Commercial benchmarks. Vendor descriptions: [S5] TOMRA FINDER (2026) and [S6] STEINERT KSS CLI. Optical, induction and geometry combinations already exist.

S7–S10. Space research context. [S7] Pham et al. (2021) reports six-degree-of-freedom simulation and physical water-supported planar manipulation. [S8] Tabor et al. (2025) uses a water-supported planar testbed and discusses small forces and distance limits. [S9] Allen et al. (2024) examines an aluminium sphere in a one-axis low-friction detumbling rig. [S10] Ortiz Gómez and Walker (2017) models coupled spacecraft/target dynamics. These are laboratory or modelling results, not Ferrosift flight performance; reaction and inertia statements are physical deductions.
