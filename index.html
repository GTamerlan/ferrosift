01 / OUR APPROACHGEOMETRY + CONDUCTIVE RESPONSE

MORE USEFUL CLUES.
BETTER ROUTING.

Boards, insulated cables and mixed metal fragments can carry copper together with other valuable material. We want to help route each whole piece to a useful destination.

Our hypothesis combines induction from multiple directions with a camera and proposed optional depth sensing. We will test it against strong single-direction spectral induction and optical baselines on independent pieces and source lots. [S1–S4]

Added hardware is worth pursuing only if it improves the collected output enough to justify its cost. No Revalis advantage has yet been demonstrated.

Study details, per-tonne arithmetic & annual value assumptions +

Trial method. An RGB camera and computer vision identified pieces for air-jet ejection; the output streams were then manually sorted and weighed. This is a published optical-sorting trial, not Revalis or multi-direction induction data.

Measured batch. The paper calls the final run nominally 490 kg. Its reported outputs sum to 490.92 kg: 452.98 kg accepted, including 0.42 kg visibly copper-bearing objects; 37.94 kg rejected, comprising 3.55 kg copper-bearing objects, 33.84 kg iron and 0.55 kg other material.

What the figures mean. Missed copper-bearing-object mass is 0.42 ÷ (3.55 + 0.42) × 100 = 10.58%, rounded to 10.6%; copper-bearing-object mass rejection is 89.42%. Diverted iron is 33.84 ÷ 490.92 × 100 = 6.893% of the whole measured batch, rounded to 6.9%. These are different denominators, not additive error rates or elemental copper recovery. The paper’s 99.907% headline describes visible-object mass purity, not chemical purity. It reports some iron-wire classification errors but does not allocate all diverted iron among classification, pneumatic bycatch and timing.

Illustrative target per tonne. Using total measured outputs as the denominator: 33.84 ÷ 490.92 × 1,000 = 68.9318015155 kg of diverted iron per tonne. A hypothetical 50% reduction retains 34.4659007578 kg/t (about 34.47 kg/t), assuming unchanged copper-bearing-object rejection.

Illustrative gross value before costs. At an assumed 10,000 tonnes/year of compatible feed with this composition, that is about 344.7 tonnes/year retained in the preferred product. At an assumed US$100/tonne incremental value spread between that product and the existing reject destination, the calculation is (33.84 ÷ 490.92 × 0.5) × 10,000 × US$100 = US$34,465.90076/year, or about US$34,466/year, before all added costs.

Both destinations retain value; we use their incremental value difference, not the full metal selling price. Feed volume, the 50% target and value spread are assumptions—not device throughput, a price quote or measured Revalis gains. Added equipment, installation, operation, maintenance, energy, financing and other costs could erase the gross value. A steel-scrap trial does not establish results for all e-waste or commercial sorters.

Research funding will support the instruments, engineering and independent trials needed to decide whether this module earns a place on a recycling line.

01

ESTABLISH THE MEASUREMENT

Build and characterize the paired sensing heads, synchronized acquisition and camera calibration. Measure how geometry, belt background, nearby metal and temperature affect the signals.

Evidence to produce: repeatable measurements and a mapped useful aperture.

02

TEST THE ADDED INFORMATION

Compare multi-direction induction, camera and proposed optional depth against strong single-direction spectral induction and optical baselines on independent fragments and source lots, with material analysis and buyer-defined classes.

Evidence to produce: a reproducible improvement on a valuable sorting decision.

03

PROVE THE INTEGRATION

Engineer maintainable mounts, calibration and host interfaces. Then test the collected output, operating burden and net value with line owners and integrators.

Evidence to produce: practical deployment conditions and operator economics.

Each stage will earn the next. A stronger induction response alone will not establish copper purity or total value; buyer requirements and the composition of the complete fragment will determine the useful route.

Methods, assumptions & design status

Historical conveyor comparison. The selected historical Ferrosift YOLO26s configuration detected 7,081 of 7,535 visible-copper annotations (93.9748%); the YOLO11s reference detected 6,448 (85.5740%). The difference is 633 annotation detections, or 8.4008 percentage points, across 1,522 development frames. Model configuration and thresholds were selected using this cohort. Repeated appearances of the same piece count as repeated annotations; this is development evidence, not a fresh independent holdout or a measure of unique recovered objects.

Operating point and comparison scope. Candidate and reference use frozen thresholds of 0.8073331713676453 and 0.9101877808570862. Candidate copper alarms matched 361 steel annotations and left 3 unmatched alarms; the reference matched 364 steel annotations and left 1 unmatched alarm. Their respective totals of 364 and 365 non-copper alarms are each approximately 1% of 36,563 steel annotations. This conservative development alarm-burden cap is distinct from conventional false-positive rate and physical steel loss. The comparison reflects complete selected configurations: architecture, training and selection effects are not isolated. The reference is an internal model, not a commercial sorter. Recorded counts and provenance · Evaluation methods.

Video interpretation. Source footage, author overlays and historical Ferrosift predictions are identified separately in the video comparison. Historical SteelDS source exposure and repeated object pools remain relevant. Playback speed, sampled prediction cadence and measured inference speed are separate quantities. New material types, camera views, lighting, occlusion and belt loads require their own evaluation; detection results do not measure recovered mass, purity or ejection success.

Module research and design status. The combined module is a development hypothesis. Testing will compare simpler configurations on independent pieces and source lots, then assess material analysis, collected purity, recovery and net value. Induction will support routing rather than direct elemental assay. CAD dimensions, coil layouts and installation envelopes are proposed; the model is preliminary and not fabrication-certified. The 180 mm lane is a mechanical test passage, not measured sensor coverage. The initial 12.5–50 mm feed target requires a separate height, mass and preparation specification.

Installation and timing. Two separated induction stations observe a fragment sequentially. Belt encoder counts do not measure fragment slip, roll or bounce; object association, calibration, useful field coverage, cross-talk, actual belt and host-metal effects, loaded clearances and downstream timing require tests. The module excludes host feeding, ejection and collection equipment. Engineering holds · Host interfaces.

Planning scenario. The illustrative target halves the iron diversion in one published steel-scrap batch while holding copper-bearing-object rejection unchanged by assumption. Per-tonne and annual gross-value arithmetic uses measured output mass, assumed compatible feed volume and an incremental value spread between destinations. It is not measured Revalis value, and no CV score contributes dollars. The original reader-assumption calculator remains available separately. The engineering program describes teams and work we plan to build.
