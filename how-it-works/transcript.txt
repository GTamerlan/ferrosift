FERROSIFT — HOW IT WORKS
Original illustrative concept. 50-second silent guide.

0–5s | First, find the fragment.
The camera detects a piece and separates its visible outline from the belt.
An outline describes appearance. It does not reveal composition.

5–10s | Give it a temporary identity.
Software calls this fragment “42” and links its observations as it travels.
42 is a temporary ID for this tracked piece—not a physical tag or proof of correct matching.

10–15s | Add geometry, if depth is available.
Optional depth sensing could estimate surface height and pose to help account for distance and orientation.
Proposed, unselected sensor. Surface geometry is not hidden-metal depth or automatic calibration.

15–20s | Predict when 42 reaches each station.
An encoder measures belt travel. Software predicts station arrival.
A prediction still needs an association check: pieces can slip, overlap or rotate.

20–27s | A changing magnetic field induces currents.
AC in the coil creates a changing magnetic field. It induces closed current loops inside conducting scrap.
No current jumps from a coil or cover into scrap. A nonconductive sensing window is not metal shielding.

27–33s | Measure the electrical response.
The fragment’s secondary field changes receive-coil voltage. Timing and tracking would associate a reading with piece 42.
Amplitude and phase are relative to the drive. Calibration would use known samples; no measurements are shown.

33–39s | Try another field direction.
For this assumed patch, the transverse magnetic field crosses its face more directly. The response could be stronger.
Fixed pose; conceptual cutaway, not an interior scan. Actual response depends on transmitter and receiver geometry.

39–45s | Combine observations. Keep uncertainty.
Camera appearance, optional surface geometry and directional responses would inform a routing class and uncertainty.
Copper and aluminium both conduct. Pose and distance change response; stronger does not mean more copper.

45–50s | Send a decision to the existing sorter.
The host receives a proposed route—or recheck—with the piece ID and timing information.
The host actuator, collection and recovery must be tested. This is an unvalidated concept.

Arrows are illustrative, not solved fields. Orange loops are electrical currents inside a generic conductor. Blue arrows represent magnetic field in air. No measured response, classification, assay, value, coverage or validated performance is shown.

Association can fail through overlap, occlusion, slip, bounce or rotation. Encoder travel alone is not proof of object identity. Another field direction must earn its cost against simpler baselines. The existing host sorter and collection require end-to-end validation.
