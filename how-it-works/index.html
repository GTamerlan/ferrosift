    What the illustration leaves out

    Piece 42 is a temporary software identity. Overlap, occlusion, slipping, bouncing or rotation can break the association between camera and induction stations. Encoder travel alone does not prove which object produced a response. Ambiguous records should be flagged or rechecked.

    Blue arrows symbolize a changing magnetic field in air. They are not electric current, a beam, measured coverage or a computed field solution. Orange closed loops symbolize induced electrical current inside a generic conductor. Real currents depend on material, geometry, frequency and surrounding conductors; the exact paths and phase are not shown.

    The receiver measures voltage, from which amplitude and phase relative to the drive may be extracted. Direct transmitter coupling, drift, background response and target–host interaction require characterization. No waveform, class, confidence, assay, copper content or value in this guide is measured.

    The concept has six unoptimized functional windings: two transmitters and four candidate receivers, arranged for two intended directions. The simplified icons do not reproduce winding details. Another direction may provide useful information; it may also fail to justify extra hardware. Two directions are not tomography or recovery of a general response tensor.

    Camera and optional depth sensing observe exterior geometry. Induction response depends on conductivity and other physical properties as well as shape and pose. Neither a surface outline nor a strong response establishes composition, purity or monetary value. Proposed routing classes and uncertainty need independent trials and buyer-specific definitions.

    The actuator and collection system belong to the host sorter. End-to-end routing, purity, recovery, throughput and total cost must be measured against strong simpler baselines and the incumbent process.

    Physics sources

    The drawings and wording are original educational schematics. These primary educational sources support the induction principles, not Ferrosift performance:

    • Feynman Lectures, II–17: The Laws of Induction — a time-varying magnetic field produces a circulating electric field.
    • OpenStax University Physics, 13.5: Eddy Currents — currents induced in conductors and the transmitter/receiver metal-detector principle.
    • Zurich Instruments: Signal Processing Basics — phase-sensitive amplitude and phase measurement.
