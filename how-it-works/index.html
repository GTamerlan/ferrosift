    Learn more: measurements and limits

    Surface geometry, not hidden metal. The camera observes appearance. A proposed, unselected depth sensor could measure external surface height and pose, helping a model account for distance from the coils (lift-off) and orientation. It would not measure buried-metal depth or automatically calibrate the system.

    Calibration uses known samples. Proposed system calibration would use reference samples with controlled positions and orientations to characterize response and check drift. Hardware, frequencies and a validated calibration procedure remain to be selected and tested.

    A signal is not a copper assay. Rotating the same piece or changing its distance can alter its signal without changing its copper content. Copper and aluminium are both conductive; induction is not uniquely a copper detector. A stronger response does not necessarily mean more copper, higher purity or greater value.

    Currents form inside the scrap. Blue arrows symbolize magnetic fields in air. Orange closed loops symbolize electrical currents induced locally inside conducting scrap. No electrical current jumps through the air from a coil or metal cover into the scrap. A nonconductive sensing window is different from conductive metal shielding: nearby shielding can carry its own induced currents and alter or attenuate the field. The drawings select neither window material nor shield performance, and show no solved fields or exact current paths.

    The receiver inset is functional, not spatial. It explains receive-coil voltage and amplitude/phase relative to the drive; it does not specify receiver position or orientation. Cross-axis receiving can work in suitable geometries. Direct transmitter coupling, background response, drift and target–host interaction still require characterization. No waveform or measured signal is shown.

    Piece ID is a software label. Record 42 refers to a tracked piece. Encoder travel and tracking would associate observations across stations; the ID itself is not proof of a correct match. Overlap, occlusion, slip, bounce, rotation or missed detections can break association. Ambiguous records should be flagged or rechecked.

    Why try another direction? The side-by-side cutaway assumes one thin copper patch tilted nearly upright inside a generic fragment. Copper is specified for the example; it is not identified by the sensor. Both views keep its pose fixed. A bottom field is nearly parallel to that patch; a transverse field crosses its face more directly and may induce a larger circulating response under comparable excitation. The illustration is a hypothesis about an assumed geometry, not a reconstruction of hidden copper. Actual receive voltage depends on both transmit and receive fields, frequency, material, shape, position and background coupling. Arrow length and line weight do not encode measured magnitudes. No fixed number of extra coils guarantees detection.

    Fusion estimates a route. Camera appearance, optional surface geometry and responses from multiple field directions would inform buyer-specific routing classes and uncertainty. This is not a full internal 3D image, elemental map or assay. The candidate has six unoptimized functional windings—two transmitters and four candidate receivers—for two intended directions, not a full response tensor. No electrical specifications or usable aperture are established by these simplified icons.

    The whole process needs testing. An extra direction may fail to justify its hardware and calibration cost. Classification, association, host actuation and collected outputs must be tested against strong simpler baselines and the incumbent process at matched throughput, purity and total cost. No physical sorting performance, confidence, copper recovery or financial gain is validated here.
