Technical details, evidence & files+

Scope and open tests

A research apparatus.

Six unoptimized functional windings provide two intended directions. The 180 mm mechanical test lane on a 600 mm host reference is not validated sensing coverage. Installation, frequencies, electrical winding choices, timing and physical performance remain open.

Scrolling forward or backward moves one illustrative jagged steel fragment with an attached copper winding through the actual CAD. The view settles briefly into each scroll position; there is no automatic playback. The entire mixed piece keeps its pose at both sensing stations. Steel magnetization, copper conductivity, winding geometry, frequency, distance and calibration all affect response; another direction may add evidence but cannot guarantee copper identification. The damaged winding has visible cut leads; its electrical continuity is unspecified. Direction arrows distinguish normal belt travel from the intended sensing axis at each station. The sensing arrows are axis markers, not a field-line solution, electron motion or electrical current. No field solution, current path, signal amplitude or copper assay is calculated. The bottom close-up makes the belt and sensing window transparent. Hardware cutaways omit selected surrounding fixtures. The final host-routing scene is schematic: the whole mixed piece goes to a proposed mixed-material route, with validation or recheck required. It is not an ejector design or proof of capture. Any later liberation and separation of the attached copper belongs to a separate downstream process.

Optical, induction and inferred records remain distinct. Belt travel does not prove same-piece association; slip, rotation or overlap can require recheck. Internal wiring, calibration, host interfaces and safe integration require further engineering.

Illustrative target arithmetic

Retain more of the good material.

The published nominal 490 kg steel-scrap trial reported 490.92 kg of total outputs. The reject stream contained 33.84 kg of iron and 3.55 kg of copper-bearing objects; 0.42 kg of copper-bearing objects remained in the accepted stream.

A hypothetical 50% reduction in iron diversion retains 16.92 kg in that batch, assuming unchanged copper-bearing-object rejection. The per-tonne denominator is the 490.92 kg measured total. Any annual value uses an assumed incremental product/reject value spread, preserving residue value and excluding all added costs. These are whole-object masses, not elemental copper recovery.
